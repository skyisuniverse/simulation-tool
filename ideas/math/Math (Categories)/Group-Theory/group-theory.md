## The Definition of a Group

A group consists of a set (G) paired with a binary operation (⋅) that combines any two elements to form a third element, subject to four fundamental axioms:

[Group Theory | Brilliant Math & Science Wiki](https://brilliant.org/wiki/group-theory-introduction/)

[Group theory | Definition, Axioms, & Applications | Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/science/group-theory)

- **Closure**: For any two elements a and b in G, the product a ⋅ b is also in G.
- **Associativity**: For all a, b, c in G, \((a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)\).
- **Identity Element**: There exists an element e in G such that e ⋅ a = a ⋅ e = a for every a in G.
- **Inverse Element**: For each a in G, there is an inverse element a⁻¹ such that a ⋅ a⁻¹ = a⁻¹ ⋅ a = e.

If the group also satisfies the commutative property (a ⋅ b = b ⋅ a), it is called an **abelian group**.

## Common Examples

- **Integers under addition** (\(\mathbb{Z}, +\)): The identity is 0, and the inverse of n is -n.
- **Symmetry groups**: The rotated and reflected positions of geometric shapes (like a square, equilateral triangle, or a molecule like CCl₄) form a group under function composition.
- **Permutation groups** (\(S_{n}\)): The set of all possible ways to shuffle or reorder n objects.

---

## Why Group Theory Matters

- **Symmetry & Physics**: Physical laws and quantum mechanics rely on symmetry groups (such as Lie groups and the Standard Model's gauge groups) to classify elementary particles and explain conservation laws via Noether's theorem.

- **Chemistry**: Chemists use molecular symmetry groups to predict polarity, vibrational spectra, and bonding properties.

- **Cryptography**: Elliptic curve groups and finite field structures provide the mathematical backbone for secure digital communications and public-key encryption.
