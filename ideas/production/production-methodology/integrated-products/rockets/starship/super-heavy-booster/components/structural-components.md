# Structural Components. Super Heavy Booster

Gemini on [List of structural components of SpaceX Starship (Super Heavy Booster)](https://share.google/aimode/EcKIrfRrHfCwjbVcB)

The [SpaceX Super Heavy booster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Super_Heavy) is built from **304L stainless steel** and organized into four primary sections from top to bottom: 

- **the interstage**, 
- **the liquid methane tank**, 
- **the liquid oxygen tank**,
- and **the aft engine section**.

## 1. Forward Section & Interstage

- **Hot-Staging Interstage**. Connects the booster to the upper-stage Starship. On earlier blocks (V1/V2), this was a separate vented steel ring. On **Block 3 (V3)** boosters, it is permanently integrated into the booster using open structural trusses to let exhaust escape during stage separation.
- **Forward Dome & Heat Shield**. A stamped, welded steel dome that caps the top of the methane tank. On V3 boosters, it is topped with asymmetrical formed steel heat-shield plates to protect against upper-stage engine plumes during hot-staging.
- **Grid Fins**. Large, electrically actuated steel aerodynamic control surfaces mounted near the top of the booster (four fins on V1/V2; three larger, lower-mounted fins on V3) used to steer during atmospheric descent.
- **Catch Pins / Hardpoints**. Reinforced structural points near the interstage that engage with the launch tower's "chopstick" arms during mid-air catches.

## 2. Primary Hull & Propellant Tanks

- **Barrel Rings**. Stacked and robotically welded cylinders made from ~4 mm thick 304L stainless steel. Most rings are 9 meters in diameter and 1.8 meters tall.
- **Internal Stringers**. Longitudinal stiffening ribs (roughly 74 to 96 per tank) welded to the interior walls to prevent buckling under axial and aerodynamic loads.
- **Liquid Methane (CH₄) Tank**. The upper main propellant tank, bounded by the forward dome and the common bulkhead.
- **Common Bulkhead**. A shared, elliptical steel dome that separates the upper liquid methane tank from the lower liquid oxygen tank to save weight and height.
- **Liquid Oxygen (LOX) Tank**. The lower main propellant tank, spanning from the common bulkhead down to the aft bulkhead.
- **Methane Transfer Tube (Downcomer)**. A large central pipe running straight through the middle of the LOX tank to deliver liquid methane from the upper tank down to the engines.

## 3. External & Secondary Structural Systems

- **Aerodynamic Chines (Raceways)**. Long external fairings running vertically along the hull that provide aerodynamic lift/stability during descent and house wiring, pressurization lines, and COPVs (Composite Overwrapped Pressure Vessels).
- **Autogenous Pressurization Piping**. External and internal stainless steel lines that route warm gas from the engines back into the tanks to maintain structural pressure.
- **Header / Landing Tanks**. Smaller dedicated propellant reserves (such as a central LOX header tank) that ensure uninterrupted propellant flow during the flip maneuver and landing burn.

## 4. Aft Section & Thrust Structure

- **Aft Bulkhead**. A heavily reinforced, shorter steel dome sealing the bottom of the LOX tank.
- **Thrust Puck (Engine Plate)**. A thick, machined steel structural plate at the center of the aft bulkhead that absorbs the compressive forces of the inner 13 gimbaled Raptor engines.
- **Outer Engine Mount Ring & Aft Sleeve**. Four shorter (1.4 m) reinforced barrel rings and a perimeter mounting ring that support the outer ring of 20 fixed Raptor engines.
- **Propellant Manifolds & Feedlines**. Heavy-duty distribution plumbing routing LOX and methane from the tank sumps and downcomer into all 33 Raptor engines.
- **Engine Shielding**. Thermal and blast protection plates around the engine bells to shield hardware from extreme heat and acoustic loads during ascent and reentry.
