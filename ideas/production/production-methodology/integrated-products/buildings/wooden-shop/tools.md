# Tools

From conversation with Gemini on [List of tools for building a wooden shop](https://share.google/aimode/djitrvwQschJKTJQy)

Building a wooden shop solo requires a mix of: 
- **framing**, 
- **cutting**, 
- **fastening**, 
- and **leveling tools**

to handle heavy materials safely by yourself.
Here is the complete tool list broken down by phase:


## 1. Measuring & Layout

- **Tape Measure (8m / 25ft)**: Use one with a wide, stiff blade and a magnetic hook so it stays attached to surfaces while you work alone.
- **Speed Square**: Essential for marking quick 90-degree and 45-degree cuts on framing lumber.
- **Framing Square**: Used for checking large right angles on walls, floors, and roof rafters.
- **4-Foot (120cm) Spirit Level**: Needed to ensure walls and door frames are straight and plumb.
- **Chalk Line**: Lets one person snap long, straight layout lines across plywood sheets and floor joists.
- **Carpenter’s Pencils & Utility Knife**: For clear markings and scoring materials.

## 2. Cutting & Shaping

- **Circular Saw (7-1/4" / 185mm)**: The primary workhorse for cutting dimensional lumber (2x4s, 2x6s) and ripping plywood sheets.
- **Miter Saw (Compound or Sliding)**: Speeds up repetitive, accurate crosscuts and angled cuts for framing, trim, and siding.
- **Jigsaw**: Necessary for cutting curves, notches, or openings for electrical boxes and pipes.
- **Hand Saw**: Useful for finishing cuts in tight corners where power saws cannot reach.

## 3. Fastening & Assembly

- **Cordless Drill/Driver**: For pre-drilling pilot holes and driving smaller screws.
- **Impact Driver**: Essential for driving long structural screws and lag bolts without straining your wrist.
- **Framing Hammer (20–24 oz)**: A straight-claw, milled-face hammer for driving framing nails and prying wood apart.
- **Nail Gun (Framing & Brad Nailers)**: Optional, though a cordless or pneumatic nailer saves massive time and fatigue when building solo.
- **Heavy-Duty Staple Gun**: Used for attaching house wrap (vapor barrier), insulation, and roofing felt.

## 4. Solo-Helper & Clamping Gear

- **Bar Clamps & C-Clamps (at least 4–6)**: Act as your "second pair of hands" to hold boards in place while you fasten them.
- **Sawhorses (2–4)**: Provide a waist-high, stable work surface for cutting wood safely.
- **Pry Bar / Crowbar**: Gives you leverage to nudge heavy walls into alignment or pull out misplaced nails.
- **Ratchet Straps**: Helpful for pulling bowed walls or roof trusses together temporarily while you nail them.

## 5. Finishing & Safety

- **Random Orbital Sander**: For smoothing rough edges, shelves, and wooden countertops.
- **Caulking Gun**: For sealing gaps around windows, doors, and exterior siding.
- **Ladder (6ft Step Ladder & Extension Ladder)**: Needed to safely reach wall top plates and roof framing.
- **Safety Gear**: Safety glasses, hearing protection (earmuffs/plugs), work gloves, and a dust mask.
