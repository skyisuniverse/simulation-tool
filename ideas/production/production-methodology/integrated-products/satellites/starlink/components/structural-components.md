# Structural Components. Upper Stage (Ship)

Gemini on [List of structural components of SpaceX Starship (Upper Stage - Ship)](https://share.google/aimode/GpeDGQoHikRzl6AvG)

The **SpaceX Starship upper stage (Ship)** is constructed from **300-series stainless steel rings and domes** using a semi-monocoque design, where pressurized propellant tanks provide primary structural rigidity.

From top to bottom, its major structural components are organized into the following sections:


## 1. Nose Cone & Payload Section

- **Nose Cone Tip & Cap**. A rounded stainless steel cap forms the forward apex of the vehicle and houses the Liquid Oxygen (LOX) header tank.
- **Tapered Nose Cone Rings**. Welded stainless steel barrel sections that taper inward to create the aerodynamic ogive profile.
- **Payload Bay**. A large, unpressurized internal volume (~614 to 1,000 m³ depending on the configuration) designed to house satellites, cargo, or a pressurized crew cabin.
- **Payload Deployment Door**. A structural hatch (such as the "Pez dispenser" slot for Starlink satellites or a larger clamshell door) cut into the upper hull and reinforced with surrounding structural frames.
- **Catch Pins / Lifting Points**. Reinforced hardpoints located near the forward section that allow the launch tower's "chopsticks" arms to lift, stack, and catch the returning spacecraft.


## 2. Primary Hull & Reinforcement

- **Main Barrel Rings**. Stacked 9-meter-diameter stainless steel cylinders (each roughly 1.83 meters tall and ~4 mm thick) welded together to form the outer skin and tank walls.
- **Internal Stringers & Ring Frames**. Vertical ribs (stringers) and circumferential stiffener rings welded to the interior of the steel skin to prevent buckling under axial compression and aerodynamic loads.
- **Raceways**. External aerodynamic fairings running vertically along the hull to house and protect avionics wiring, data cables, and propellant lines.

## 3. Propellant Tankage (Cryogenic)

- **Forward Dome**. An elliptical stainless steel bulkhead that seals the top of the main Liquid Methane (CH₄) tank and separates it from the payload bay.
- **Main Liquid Methane (CH₄) Tank**. The upper primary propellant tank formed directly by the stainless steel outer hull.
- **Common Dome**. An inverted elliptical bulkhead separating the upper main methane tank from the lower main liquid oxygen tank.
- **Main Liquid Oxygen (LOX) Tank**. The lower primary propellant tank occupying the mid-to-aft fuselage.
- **Aft Dome**. The lower elliptical bulkhead that closes the bottom of the main LOX tank and transfers thrust loads from the engine section into the hull.
- **Header Tanks**. Two smaller auxiliary tanks used to hold landing propellant during atmospheric entry and the landing flip:
	- **LOX Header Tank**: Located inside the very tip of the nose cone to help balance the vehicle's center of mass during belly-flop descent.
	- **Methane (CH₄) Header Tank**: Integrated directly into the center of the common dome.
- **Downcomer & Transfer Tubes**. Internal piping that routes cryogenic propellants from the forward tanks and header tanks down through the lower tank to the engines.


## 4. Aerodynamic & Thermal Protection Structures

- **Forward Flaps**. Two smaller hinged aerodynamic control surfaces mounted symmetrically near the nose cone to control pitch and roll during atmospheric reentry.
- **Aft Flaps**. Two larger hinged control surfaces mounted near the base of the ship for primary aerodynamic braking and attitude control.
- **Flap Aero-Covers / Fairings**. Streamlined stainless steel housings that shield the hinges, electric actuators, and drive mechanisms of the flaps from hypersonic plasma.
- **Thermal Protection System (TPS)**. Thousands of hexagonal silica-based ceramic heat shield tiles mounted across the windward ("belly") side, nose cone, and leading edges of the flaps to withstand reentry temperatures exceeding 1,400 °C.


## 5. Aft Skirt & Propulsion Structure

- **Aft Skirt**. The unpressurized cylindrical extension of the hull below the aft dome that shields the engines and connects the ship to the Super Heavy booster's hot-staging ring.
- **Thrust Puck / Engine Mount Structure**. A heavy, reinforced structural plate and web assembly beneath the aft dome that absorbs the thrust of the engines and distributes it evenly into the skirt and tank walls.
- **Engine Gimbal Mounts & Actuators**. Structural mounts and electromechanical actuators that allow the three center engines to pivot for thrust vector control.
- **Raptor Propulsion Cluster**. Six methane-fueled engines housed within the skirt:
	- **3 Sea-Level Raptor (RSL) Engines**: Mounted centrally with gimbal capability for atmospheric flight and the landing flip.
	- **3 Raptor Vacuum (RVac) Engines**: Fixed-mount engines with large nozzle extensions optimized for orbital insertion in space.
- **COPVs (Composite Overwrapped Pressure Vessels)**. High-pressure tanks mounted inside the aft skirt and payload bay used to store gases (such as nitrogen or helium) for cold-gas attitude control thrusters and tank pressurization.
