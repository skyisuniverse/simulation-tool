**Directed energy deposition with an arc (DED-Arc)**—commonly known as **Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing (WAAM)**—is a **metal 3D printing process that uses an electric arc to melt metal wire layer-by-layer onto a substrate or existing part**. Built on established arc-welding methods like GMAW/MIG, CMT, TIG/GTAW, and plasma, it excels at making large metal parts quickly and repairing worn components.

## How It Works

- **Heat & Feedstock**: A robotic welding torch or arm moves along a programmed toolpath, using an electric arc to melt a continuous metal wire (filler material) as it deposits onto a base plate or part.

- **Layer-by-Layer Buildup**: Each pass forms a weld bead that solidifies into a layer, stacking up to create near-net-shape geometries, cavities, or internal channels.

## Key Advantages & Trade-Offs

- **High Deposition Rates & Large Scale**: Deposition rates often reach several kilograms per hour (commonly ~2–15 kg/h depending on setup), making it practical for big components limited mainly by robot reach or track length.

- **Lower Material & Equipment Cost**: Uses standard welding wire rather than expensive metal powders, and can leverage existing robotic welding hardware. [Direct Energy Deposition - Laser vs. Arc vs. Electron Beam DED](https://ewi.org/capabilities/additive-manufacturing/what-is-direct-energy-deposition/)

- **Part Repair**: Well suited for adding features or rebuilding damaged industrial parts, dies, and heavy components. [From Repairing Parts to Building Rockets! Understanding DED 3D Printing | 3D Explained | 3Dnatives](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kHnhf9Uzp2k)

- **Lower Fine Detail & Thermal Distortion**: Less suited for tiny, intricate features than powder-bed methods, and repeated heating/cooling can cause residual stress or distortion that requires path planning and thermal control. [A Comparative Study of Directed Energy Deposition‐Arc and Casting: Enhancing Mechanical and Thermal Properties of Hot‐Work Tool Steels](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394983529_A_Comparative_Study_of_Directed_Energy_Deposition-Arc_and_Casting_Enhancing_Mechanical_and_Thermal_Properties_of_Hot-Work_Tool_Steels)

## Common Materials & Uses

- **Materials**: Carbon and low-alloy steels, stainless steels, aluminum alloys, nickel superalloys (like Inconel), and copper/bronze alloys.
- **Applications**: Large marine and structural parts (such as impellers, clamps, and bridges), aerospace and heavy machinery components, tooling, and industrial part repair.
