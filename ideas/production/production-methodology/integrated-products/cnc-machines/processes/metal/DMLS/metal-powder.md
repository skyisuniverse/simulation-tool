# Metal powder

From [DMLS-Direct-Metal-Laser-Sintering.md](ideas/production/production-methodology/integrated-products/cnc-machines/processes/metal/DMLS/DMLS-Direct-Metal-Laser-Sintering.md)

> **Direct Metal Laser Sintering (DMLS)** is a metal 3D printing (powder bed fusion) process that uses a high-powered laser to **fuse fine metal powder** layer by layer from a 3D CAD model inside an inert gas chamber. Often used interchangeably with Selective Laser Melting (SLM), it produces dense, functional metal parts without molds or tooling.

> - **Powder layering**: A recoater spreads a thin layer of **metal powder** (typically tens of micrometers thick) across a build platform.

> What is fine metal powder?

**Fine metal powder** in DMLS is a specialized, flowable raw material made of microscopic, engineered metal particles—typically **15 to 45 micrometers (µm) in diameter** (finer than table salt and comparable to talcum powder or human hair)—specifically manufactured to spread evenly and melt cleanly under a laser.


## Key Characteristics

- **Spherical shape**: Unlike crushed or irregular metal dust, DMLS powder is made of near-perfect microscopic spheres so the particles roll over each other smoothly like tiny ball bearings when spread by the recoater blade.
- **Tight particle size distribution (PSD)**: Powders are sieved to a narrow size range (most commonly 15–45 µm or 20–63 µm). This allows smaller particles to nestle into the gaps between larger ones, increasing packing density so the finished part has fewer voids.
- **High purity and low oxygen**: The powder must have very low surface oxidation and minimal moisture; otherwise, trapped oxides cause defects and poor bonding during laser melting.

## How It Is Made

Because standard metal shavings or ground dust are too jagged and oxidized for DMLS, manufacturers use specialized atomization processes:

1. **Gas atomization (most common)**: Solid metal is melted in a vacuum or inert furnace and poured through a nozzle, where high-pressure jets of **argon or nitrogen** blast the molten stream into millions of tiny droplets that solidify into spheres as they fall.

2. **Plasma atomization**: Metal wire is fed into ultra-hot plasma torches, melting and spraying it into exceptionally smooth, high-purity spheres (often used for reactive aerospace alloys like **titanium Ti6Al4V**).


## Reuse and Handling

- **Recyclability**: Unmelted powder left in the build chamber after a print is vacuumed out, passed through a fine mesh sieve to remove clumps or laser spatter, blended with fresh "virgin" powder, and reused across multiple builds.

- **Safety**: Because the particles are so fine and have a huge surface-area-to-volume ratio, airborne metal powders are respiratory hazards and combustible dust risks (especially aluminum and titanium), requiring sealed handling systems, respirators, and grounding.
