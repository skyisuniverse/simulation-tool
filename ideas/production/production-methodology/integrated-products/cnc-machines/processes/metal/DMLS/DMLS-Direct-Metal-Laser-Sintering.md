# Direct Metal Laser Sintering

(short form - **Metal Sintering**)

From conversation with Gemini on [Direct Metal Laser Sintering (DMLS)](https://share.google/aimode/1niPo2sOERijowr3s)

**Direct Metal Laser Sintering (DMLS)** is a metal 3D printing (powder bed fusion) process that uses a high-powered laser to fuse fine metal powder layer by layer from a 3D CAD model inside an inert gas chamber. Often used interchangeably with Selective Laser Melting (SLM), it produces dense, functional metal parts without molds or tooling.


## How It Works

- **Powder layering**: A recoater spreads a thin layer of metal powder (typically tens of micrometers thick) across a build platform.
- **Laser fusing**: A laser selectively scans and fuses the cross-section for that layer inside a sealed chamber filled with inert gas (like argon or nitrogen) to prevent oxidation.
- **Build and post-processing**: The platform lowers, another layer is applied, and the cycle repeats. Finished parts usually undergo stress-relief heat treatment, support removal, powder cleanup, and optional CNC machining or surface finishing.

## Common Materials

- Stainless steel and tool steel
- Aluminum alloys (such as AlSi10Mg)
- Titanium alloys (such as Ti6Al4V)
- Nickel superalloys (such as Inconel), cobalt-chrome, copper, and tungsten

## Key Advantages and Limitations

- **Advantages**: Handles complex internal channels, lattices, and part consolidation; requires no molds; creates strong parts with near-wrought mechanical properties.
- **Limitations**: High equipment and operating costs, build-volume limits, thermal stress/warping risks, support structures needed for overhangs, and rougher as-printed surfaces.

## Common Applications

- **Aerospace and energy**: Lightweight brackets, turbine components, and complex ducts.

- **Medical and dental**: Custom implants, surgical guides, and dental crowns or frameworks.

- **Automotive and tooling**: Functional prototypes, low-volume production parts, and conformal cooling molds.
