## Physical AI

From [lincolnelectric.com XpertLINC](https://www.lincolnelectric.com/en/Products/Automation/XpertLINC)

> Many AI-driven solutions rely primarily on learned behavior. They collect data, train a model and use previous outcomes to determine how the system should respond. When a new part, joint condition, or source of variability is introduced, additional iterations may be required before the system reaches a production-ready result.
>
> XpertLINC takes a fundamentally different approach. It combines 
> - Lincoln Electric welding expertise, 
> - factual weld data, 
> - physics-based mathematics, 
> - and engineering principles. 
>
> The system does not simply recognize what worked before. It applies the welding science behind why a response is appropriate.
>
> That foundation allows XpertLINC to evaluate the joint that is actually presented, develop coordinated robot motion and weld parameters, and execute the weld inside the working ranges established for the customer-authorized WPS. ### CAD models / actual geometry  (for welding)

> Do I need CAD models to use XpertLINC?
> 
> No. XpertLINC eliminates the need for CAD-based programming entirely. Other automation systems depend on digital models that rarely match real-world parts. XpertLINC uses sensor scans and Physical AI to generate correct weld paths and parameters automatically, directly from actual geometry.

From [When Intelligence Meets the Weld: Physical AI in Robotic Welding](https://www.lincolnelectric.com/en/Welding-and-Cutting-Resource-Center/Arc-Magazine/Articles/2026/11/When-Intelligence-Meets-the-Weld)

> Welding automation operates in the physical world, where joint geometry, fit-up, part placement, and process variables can change what occurs at the arc. A robotic welding system can follow a programmed path with exceptional repeatability, but repeatable motion alone does not account for every difference found in an actual part.
>
> Finding the joint is not the same as understanding the weld.
>
> The XpertLINC™ Platform applies Physical AI to bridge that gap. It combines sensing, welding-process control, and more than 100 years of Lincoln Electric expertise to bring welding intelligence directly into robotic automation.
